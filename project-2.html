Analysis of Urban Form

St. Augustine, Florida, United States | 1770

St. Augustine, being the oldest European-founded city in the United States that still remains populous today, was developed differently than a lot of other modern American cities The typical building styles showcase beautiful Spanish colonialism, and the Plaza de Constitución serves as the heart of the city. In this plaza, the community can gather for any and all matters. Following Spanish colonial urban development, the city extends outwards with this multifaceted community gathering space as its core. Colonial influence proceeds to stand out in the narrowness of the streets and the lack of space between buildings and sidewalks. On another historical note, the Castillo de San Marcos stands out as a grand, bold structure that is now recognized as a national mnument. St. Augustine‘s Spanish Colonial influence and history still shines through to this day with its building styles, urban planning, and historical landmarks. Creating my own potrayal of this beautiful city with such substantial history was an exceptional way to learn about formative urban development.

Tracing Exercises

The tracing exercises assigned each class were exceedingly beneficial in several ways as a budding architect. Tracing every morning once the studio period began was an efficient way to get the mind thinking like an architect without needing much critical thinking to begin. Following along each line with my pen awoke pieces of my mind that made me curious. I asked myself questions that a new architect would, such as, „What material am I depicting with this line style?“ or even something as vague as, „What exactly am I drawing?“ Through these exercises, I learned how to read floor plans and sections, draw greenery and staircases, and study cities and buildings before analyzing and producing my own detailed versions of them.
